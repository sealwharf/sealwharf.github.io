The idea

Your certificate stays home. Only the messages travel.

Signing portals ask you to upload the document, and sometimes the certificate. Sealwharf turns this around: the signing happens in a container on your computer, and WhatsApp is only the way documents come in and go out. Whapi carries the messages and a Cloudflare tunnel gives the bot a fixed address; neither of them ever sees a certificate or a password.

What it is

A signing desk that lives in your chat

Signing a PDF with a digital certificate usually means a desktop program, a USB token plugged into the right computer and a few minutes of clicking to place the signature. When the document arrives on the phone, it has to travel to that computer first.

Sealwharf is a small bot that you run yourself. Forward the document to its number and it finds where to sign by looking for the signer's name next to an empty signature line, signs it with the right certificate and sends the PDF back. When the certificate belongs to someone else, that person approves with a single reply.

Who it is for

Professionals who sign every day

Contracts, reports and forms arrive by phone. Forward them to the bot and receive them signed, without opening a signing program.

Assistants who prepare documents

An assistant sends the document; the certificate owner gets it on their own phone and approves with 1. Nobody shares a password.

Offices that need a record

Every signature is recorded with the time, who asked, who approved and the fingerprint of the document, in a file that stays with you.

How it works

From a chat message to a signed PDF

Everything between receiving the file and sending it back happens inside the container, on your computer.

  1. 1

    Receive

    Whapi delivers the message through the Cloudflare tunnel. Numbers that are not in the contacts are ignored.

  2. 2

    Prepare

    Word documents become PDF. The image ships free fonts with the same widths as Arial, Times New Roman, Calibri and Cambria, so the layout holds.

  3. 3

    Locate

    The bot looks for the signer's full name next to an empty signature line. Capitals and accents do not matter; spelling does.

  4. 4

    Authorize

    Owners and trusted contacts are signed at once. Requests from preparers wait for the owner's reply.

  5. 5

    Sign

    The PDF is signed with the certificate file or the USB token, with a visible stamp. Signatures already in the PDF stay valid.

  6. 6

    Record

    The signed PDF goes back through the chat, and the signature is written to the audit record.

Terms used on this page
Digital certificate
An electronic identity issued by a certificate authority, such as an ICP-Brasil A1 (a .p12 or .pfx file) or A3 (on a USB token or smart card).
PAdES
The standard for signatures embedded in PDF files. Any PDF reader that checks signatures can validate them.
PKCS#11
The standard interface that programs use to talk to USB tokens and smart cards. Each vendor ships a library for it.
Whapi.Cloud
A service that connects a WhatsApp number to a program through an API. Sealwharf uses it to receive and send messages.
Cloudflare Tunnel
A connection from your computer to Cloudflare that gives the bot a public HTTPS address without opening ports on your router.
Audit record
A file with one line per event that records each signature with the time, who asked, who approved and the fingerprint of the document.

Roles

Who may use a certificate, and when the owner is asked

Each certificate has one owner. The owner decides who else may ask for a signature and whether those requests need approval.

owner

Owner

The person the certificate belongs to. Their documents are signed at once, and they approve other people's requests.

preparers

Preparers

People who prepare documents for the owner. Each request waits for the owner's approval.

trusted

Trusted

People the owner trusts completely. Their requests are signed without asking.

# config/sealwharf.yaml
certificates:
  - id: john
    name: John Doe
    file: certificates/john.p12
    password: "certificate-password"
    owner: john
    preparers: [jane]
    trusted: [mary]

An approval covers exactly the document that was sent, comes from the owner's number, is used once and expires. Because the owner answers with a plain 1 or 0, an owner never has two requests open at once.

Features

Small on the outside, careful on the inside

Everything lives in one configuration file, and one command checks it before the bot starts.

◎

Finds where to sign

Looks for the signer's name next to an empty signature line. Middle names may be initials; first and last names must be complete.

▣

Files and USB tokens

Signs with .p12 and .pfx files and with PKCS#11 tokens such as ICP-Brasil A3. Two commands find the token and print the lines to configure it.

✓

One-reply approval

When a preparer asks, the owner receives the PDF and answers 1 to approve or 0 to refuse.

⧉

Several signers

If the document names more than one signer you may use, the bot asks who should sign: one, all, or cancel. Signed PDFs can be signed again.

⌕

Signature check

Send the word verify, then a PDF, and the bot reports its signatures without signing anything.

❏

Word documents

DOCX files are converted to PDF before signing. If a document uses other fonts, copy them into config/fonts and restart.

⏱

Timestamp and trust roots

Optional RFC 3161 timestamp, and validation of the signer's chain and revocation status against the root certificates you choose.

◐

Local AI, optional

A small vision model running on your computer, through Ollama, can find signature places in forms and scanned pages.

⇄

English and Portuguese

Every chat message exists in English and Brazilian Portuguese, chosen per contact.

Safety

Built to refuse when in doubt

A wrong signature is worse than no signature. These rules hold everywhere in the code, and the tests cover the ambiguous cases.

1
PIN attempt before the bot stops, to protect the token
5 min
for a question or an approval to be answered
20
documents per number per hour, by default
30 days
of daily reminders before a certificate expires

Ambiguity means no signature

If the place to sign is unclear, or a name could belong to two signers, nothing is signed for that signer.

Approvals are narrow

An approval covers the exact document that was sent, comes from the owner's number, is used once and expires.

Secrets stay in one file

Passwords and PINs live only in the configuration file. A certificate sent through the chat is refused without being opened.

Logs without people

Operational logs carry no phone numbers, names, file names or document content.

A PIN is tried once

After a refusal, a guard file blocks every new login, even after a restart, until you run token-retry.

A locked-down container

Read-only file system, every Linux capability dropped, no privilege escalation, and the configuration folder mounted read-only.

What a signature proves, and what it does not
  • A signature proves that the document was signed with that certificate and was not changed afterwards.
  • Whether the certificate authority is trusted is checked only when trust roots are configured.
  • Numbers that are not in the contacts are ignored. Only the people you register can talk to the bot.
  • Whapi and Cloudflare carry the messages, so documents pass through them on the way in and out. Certificates and passwords never do.

Write the name, send the file

For the bot to find where to sign, the document needs the signer's full name next to an empty signature line.

José Gonçalves
Ana Lima

José Gonçalves, Jose Goncalves and JOSE GONCALVES are the same name for the bot, so a document typed without accents is signed just the same.

Quick start

Set it up in five steps

The bot runs as a Docker container. Before you begin, have these four things at hand:

  • Docker Desktop, or Docker with Compose
  • A Whapi.Cloud channel
  • A domain on Cloudflare
  • A .p12/.pfx file or a USB token

  1. 1Download the project

    Clone the repository, or download it as a ZIP file and unzip it. Run every command below from the project folder.

    git clone https://github.com/sealwharf/sealwharf.git
cd sealwharf

  2. 2Create the bot's address

    In the Cloudflare dashboard, open Networking, then Tunnels, and create a tunnel. Do not install anything: copy only the long token that starts with eyJ, and keep the page open.

  3. 3Get the WhatsApp token

    In the Whapi dashboard, open your channel, which must be connected (QR code scanned), and copy its API token.

  4. 4Fill in the configuration

    Copy your certificate into config/certificates, then copy config/sealwharf.example.yaml to config/sealwharf.yaml. This is the smallest working file:

    public_url: https://bot.example.com
whapi_token: "the token from step 3"
tunnel_token: "the token from step 2"

contacts:
  john:
    name: John Doe
    number: "5511999999999"

certificates:
  - id: john
    name: John Doe
    file: certificates/john.p12
    password: "the certificate password"
    owner: john

  5. 5Check, start and publish

    The check opens every certificate, tests the WhatsApp token and tells you exactly what to fix. When it ends with Ready to start., start the bot.

    docker compose run --rm sealwharf check
docker compose up -d

    Back in Cloudflare, add a published application route from your address to http://localhost:8000. A minute later, send BOTM4 to the bot: it answers Hello.
